THE PROBLEM · 02

The cold meeting burns the scarcest resource in the university: expert time.

You have 30 minutes with a visiting scholar, a prospective co-author, or a program officer — and no time to read their work. So the meeting starts with ten minutes of catch-up, or skips the substance entirely.

The cost is real, and absent from any budget line.

  • A faculty consulting hour benchmarks at $85–$360; a research assistant at $22–$35/hr.
  • A competent pre-meeting dossier takes an RA 2–4 hours — $70–$1,400 of staff time, and a day's lead time.
  • The alternative today is a DIY LLM prompt: 30–60 minutes of effort, no sources, inconsistent, no grant data.
  • The meeting load is rising — grant-driven and interdisciplinary work means more cross-field meetings every year.
THE PRODUCT · 03

A name in. A brief you can read on the walk over.

INA name — plus optional institution, URL, ORCID, or a pasted agenda.
OUTA 9–13 slide brief — profile & headshot, research threads, funded portfolio, recognition, and ranked collaboration talking points with a recommended opener.
OUTPDF + share link, with a source link on every claim and a "verify before you rely on it" banner.
NOTNot a CRM, not a meeting-notes tool, not a people-search database. It is the pre-meeting synthesis artifact.

The whole value is saved time and a better meeting — an artifact, not another tab of raw data.

MVP ships: name → brief, sources, PDF export, and a cache of every person generated.

WHY NOW & THE WEDGE · 04

The compute flipped, the data is free, and nobody owns the niche.

  • Compute cost collapse. Synthesis now runs for cents on mid-tier models ($2/$10 per M tokens) and returns good prose. The economics flipped.
  • Open structured data is free. OpenAlex, ORCID, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, NIH RePORTER, NSF Awards API — identity, publications, and grants at no cost.
  • The hard part moved. Finding data is solved; synthesising it legibly is the job. That is exactly what a model does well now.
  • No incumbent. Sales-intel tools serve B2B sales; academic databases serve raw data; meeting tools record after. The pre-meeting person-brief for academics is open.

UNIT ECONOMICS IV · 08

The cache is the moat — and it drives acquisition to zero.

Most briefs are about recurring subjects — famous scholars, deans, program officers, frequent collaborators. The first brief about a person costs $0.15. Every later brief about the same person costs ~$0.005 (re-format from the cached fact base; no re-research).

  • At a 40% cache-hit rate, blended COGS falls from $0.16 to ~$0.10.
  • At 60%, it falls to ~$0.07 — a structural cost advantage a new entrant cannot copy without the corpus.
  • It also makes the public profile pages essentially free to serve — the SEO flywheel.

GO-TO-MARKET · 12

The SEO flywheel is both the cost moat and the acquisition engine.

Every public figure briefed can power a public, opt-out-able profile page — capturing high-intent long-tail searches ("who is X", "X research area", "prepare for a meeting with X") at near-zero marginal cost, served from cache. Content compounds; CAC trends to zero.

Channels, in priority order
  • SEO / programmatic content — profile pages + how-to guides. The compounding asset.
  • Conference presence — SIGCSE, CSCW, CHI, ICSE, ASEE. Hook: "give us your next five meetings — we'll brief them free at the booth."
  • Research-office channel — VPR, CTL, libraries, grant offices already buy tools for faculty. Shortest path to Department/site licenses.
 
  • Academic social — X/Bluesky/Mastodon, LinkedIn. The "name in → brief out" demo GIF is inherently shareable.
  • Viral loop — every brief carries a subtle mark and share link; the recipient is a qualified prospect.
  • Partnerships — OpenAlex/ORCID/Semantic Scholar for credibility and data; university procurement as the enterprise motion.

Positioning line: "Brief yourself before the meeting. In two minutes, not two hours."

RISKS & THE ASK · 14

The plan is sound if we control three things: accuracy, legality, and free-tier cost.

  • Accuracy — source-link every claim, quality gate before charging, optional human-review tier.
  • Privacy / legality — prefer open APIs, honour robots.txt, professional info only, opt-out + noindex, takedown SLA; no personality or personal inference.
  • Free-tier cost — cap at 2/mo (≤$0.30/user exposure), per-user COGS meter, kill-switch.
  • Model cost drift — model-agnostic layer, aggressive cache, pinned cheap default.
  • Quality → churn — invest in the fact base, not the prose; let users pick the synthesis tier.
  • Incumbent copies the surface — speed to the corpus and the SEO pages.
The ask: runway to Phase 2 — ~$150–250k for 12 months (founder + one engineer + tooling + conference travel), or the equivalent as a university-partnered pilot. Start concierge. Ship the manual pipeline to 50 people before writing product code.
